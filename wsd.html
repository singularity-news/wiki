Who Wrote the Contract
That Sold the World?

World Succession Deed 1400/98 · Signed October 6th, 1998

The Federal Republic of Germany, together with NATO-related structures, the Kingdom of the Netherlands, the Dutch Air Force, and treaty-linked UN telecommunications frameworks, created one of the most complex international legal constructions ever embedded inside a property transaction.

The treaty known as World Succession Deed 1400/98 was signed on October 6th, 1998, yet according to the integrated legal structure behind the agreement, the contract was never intended to function as a normal German real estate transaction. Instead, the document was constructed as a layered international treaty framework connected to NATO, ITU, and military infrastructure networks operating across national borders.

§ 1The Buyer Did Not Write the Contract

A Nineteen-Year-Old and the Weight of History

At the beginning of the negotiations, The Buyer was only nineteen years old. He had no background in international law, military treaty systems, NATO infrastructure law, telecommunications governance, or state succession doctrine.

The Buyer originally only wanted to work as a broker for former NATO real estate and earn commission through conversion projects after the Cold War. For years, unpaid work was performed while investors, authorities, military property offices, and conversion agencies negotiated the future of the Kreuzberg area in Zweibrücken.

Only after the treaty had already passed through parliamentary structures and administrative approval processes was The Buyer confronted with a final decision: leave the project entirely — or acquire the property directly instead of receiving commission.

According to the integrated interpretation of the deed, this moment transformed a simple real estate transaction into a massive international transfer mechanism involving infrastructure, jurisdiction, treaty continuity, and military communications systems.

§ 2Koblenz Castle and the International Treaty Experts

The OFD Koblenz — NATO Property Administration

The Electoral Palace in Koblenz functioned as one of the central administrative and legal nodes of the NATO infrastructure system in Germany during the 1990s. Inside this institutional structure operated experts operating across every dimension of NATO law and military conversion:

  • NATO Status of Forces Agreement (SOFA) experts
  • International law specialists
  • Military infrastructure administrators
  • Conversion authorities
  • Federal procurement systems
  • Deployment law experts
  • Telecommunications coordinators
  • Strategic infrastructure planners
  • Military barracks and airfield management
  • Fiber, cable and utility corridor specialists

The treaty wording therefore emerged inside an environment where military logistics, international law, NATO infrastructure governance, and telecommunications administration overlapped permanently.

§ 5TKS Telepost and the Telecommunications Network

The Military–Civilian Dual-Use Network

One of the most important components connected to the legal chain was TKS Telepost / TKS Cable. TKS originated from military initiatives involving USEUCOM, USAREUR, and USAFE — the objective was to provide English-language telecommunications services for military communities across Germany and NATO-linked environments.

  • Television distribution systems
  • Telephone services
  • Broadband internet
  • Mobile communications
  • Military community cable systems
  • International routing infrastructure

TKS later operated through existing military cable and infrastructure environments linked to former U.S. Army installations. According to the treaty-chain interpretation, the continued worldwide use of these networks after signing constituted:

  • Partial fulfillment
  • Continued participation by conduct
  • Acceptance through ongoing usage
  • International recognition through usage
  • Estoppel through lack of objection

The central concern was that if The Buyer initiated legal proceedings inside the FRG system, jurisdiction could become consolidated through a legally binding court ruling.

§ 9The Contract That Connected the World

Precision Inside Every Clause

The World Succession Deed 1400/98 is interpreted as far more than a property contract. Inside the doctrinal structure, it became:

  • A state succession instrument
  • A treaty-chain activator
  • A telecommunications integration mechanism
  • A NATO-linked supplementary instrument
  • A jurisdictional transfer system
  • A network-based territorial expansion process
  • A global infrastructure consolidation framework

The contract was written by highly specialized experts operating at the intersection of international law, NATO stationing law, military infrastructure administration, telecommunications regulation, and global communications management. Every clause was formulated with precision. Nothing inside the wording appears accidental.

The treaty embedded military communications, NATO infrastructure, telecommunications systems, jurisdictional mechanisms, and international treaty continuity into one interconnected legal structure — hidden beneath the appearance of a normal German real estate contract.
